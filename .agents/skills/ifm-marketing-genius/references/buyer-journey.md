# IFM buyer journey

Committee-sold services. Marketing that speaks to one role will be vetoed by
another.

## The committee (typical multi-site deal)

| Role | Job to be done | What they fear | What they need to see |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VP / Dir Facilities | Keep sites open and on-brand | Downtime, weak SPs, 2am calls | Coverage map, first-time fix, dispatch SLAs, QA |
| Procurement | Defensible cost and contract | Invoice leakage, lock-in, audit | Should-cost, NTE discipline, transparency, exit terms |
| Finance / CFO influencer | Predictable OpEx | Surprise spend, weak ROI story | Cost-to-serve, variance reduction, invoice accuracy |
| Store / restaurant ops | Don't make my team's life worse | Missed windows, messy techs, brand hits | Arrival windows, brand standards, communication |
| Risk / legal / safety | Don't get us in the paper | Unlicensed work, COI, injuries | Compliance stack, vetting, documentation |
| IT / digital (sometimes) | Don't break our stack | Integration theater | CMMS/FMS/work-order reality, not vapor |

**Champion** is usually facilities. **Blocker** is often procurement or an
incumbent relationship. **Economic buyer** may sit above both.

## Journey stages (IFM-specific)

Do not use a PLG funnel. Use this:

1. **Trigger** — a portfolio event (see market map). They are not browsing.
2. **Category framing** — IFM vs in-house vs network vs software. If you lose
   the frame, you become a line item in someone else's RFP.
3. **Long list** — who is "national," who is "safe," who is already on the
   vendor master. Mental availability + RFP lists matter here.
4. **Short list / RFP** — proof, coverage, commercial model, references in
   *their* vertical.
5. **Validation** — site visits, data room, security, implementation plan,
   who actually shows up on night one.
6. **Transition** — the first 90 days *are* the product. Marketing that
   disappears after signature creates churn stories competitors will use.
7. **Expand** — more trades, more banners, more governance. Expand content
   is cheaper than net-new logos.

## Objections you must pre-empt in marketing

- "We already have a CMMS."
- "CBRE/JLL already does our buildings."
- "National providers are expensive and slow."
- "We'll lose local relationships."
- "Your techs won't know our brand standards."
- "Implementation will disrupt stores."
- "You'll nickel-and-dime change orders."

Each objection needs a **mechanism** (how the operating model prevents it),
not a slogan.

## Content by stage

| Stage | Asset that actually gets used |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger / framing | "IFM vs in-house vs CMMS" explainer; AEO answer blocks |
| Long list | Category one-pager; coverage proof; AI-citable definitions |
| Short list | Vertical case study with site count + operational KPIs |
| Validation | Implementation 90-day plan; security/compliance one-pager |
| Champion enablement | Internal email the facilities leader can forward to procurement |
| AE rooms | Battle cards, objection doc, ROI one-pager, RFP language pack |

If an asset cannot be forwarded inside the buying company without the
marketer in the room, it is incomplete.

## Language to steal (from buyers, not from us)

Prefer Gong and live RFP language. Until then, default to operational English:

- work order, NTE, first-time fix, callback, ETA, after-hours, licensed,
  COI, brand standard, store hours, refrigeration, HVAC, grease, roof,
  parking lot, health inspection, downtime, vendor scorecard

Avoid: synergy, transformation, delight, "seamless," "one-stop shop" without
a described operating system.
