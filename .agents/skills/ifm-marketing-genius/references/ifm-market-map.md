# IFM market map

Use this when positioning, competitive work, or category education is in play.

## What buyers mean by IFM

**Integrated facilities management** = one contracted operating system for a
portfolio of sites: hard services (HVAC, electrical, plumbing, refrigeration),
often soft services (janitorial, landscaping), plus governance (SLAs, safety,
compliance, spend, vendor performance).

Adjacent categories buyers confuse with IFM:

| Label | What it actually is | Marketing implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IFM / FM outsourcing | Outsourced operations + vendor network | Outcome + risk + coverage story |
| In-house facilities | Employed techs + local vendors | Switching cost, labor, consistency |
| Regional service providers | Strong locally, thin nationally | National coverage + brand standards |
| Single-trade national | One craft, many sites | Integration and one-throat-to-choke |
| CMMS / FMS / IWMS | Software to ticket and report | Software is not the work getting done |
| Marketplace / dispatch | Work-order routing layer | Governance, licensed capacity, QA |

Vixxo marketing must **place the product on the right shelf**. If copy sounds
like a ticketing tool, you lose to software. If copy sounds like a CRE
generalist, you lose to CBRE/JLL on brand. Win on **multi-site operational
control** for operators who live and die by store/restaurant uptime.

## Competitive set (typical)

Do not treat this list as exhaustive or ranked. Refresh from live sites when
the task is competitive.

- **CRE / IFM platforms:** CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Sodexo, ISS, ABM,
  EMCOR, Compass-adjacent FM
- **Multi-site / retail specialists and networks:** Vixxo and peers in
  restaurant/retail facilities, national account maintenance
- **Work-order / network layers:** ServiceChannel-class platforms (often a
  complement *and* a competitor for "who owns the network")
- **Status quo:** in-house FM + local contractors

Positioning rule (April Dunford): alternatives are what they would do *if
Vixxo disappeared tomorrow* — not only companies with similar homepages.

## Buying triggers (demand, not demographics)

Market when these events hit a portfolio:

- Store growth, M&A, or new banners (coverage breaks)
- Cost-out / procurement mandate (NTE, should-cost, invoice leakage)
- SLA or brand-standard failure (mystery shop, health inspection, downtime)
- SP quality collapse in a region
- Leadership change in facilities / procurement
- Risk events (safety, COI gaps, unlicensed work)
- Technology refresh (CMMS/FMS RFP that is actually an operating-model RFP)

Marketing that ignores triggers and only talks "ICP industry + employee count"
wastes ABM budget.

## Category narrative (default)

**Do not** "disrupt facilities management." Buyers in this market punish
hype.

Default story:

1. Multi-site operators are punished by variance (site-to-site, SP-to-SP).
2. Spreadsheets + local vendors + a CMMS do not create a national operating
   system.
3. Giant IFM platforms optimize for real estate portfolios that do not move
   at retail/restaurant tempo.
4. Vixxo is the operating system for that tempo: licensed capacity, dispatch
   discipline, spend visibility, brand-standard work.

Tune this against `.agents/product-marketing.md` when it exists. Never invent
proof points.

## Where category conversations happen

- IFMA World Workplace and local IFMA chapters
- NRF and restaurant/retail ops conferences
- BOMA / CRE FM tracks (incumbent turf — useful for contrast, not always
  Vixxo's richest room)
- Facilities Dive, FacilitiesNet, Building Operating Management
- LinkedIn (facilities, procurement, store ops leaders)
- RFP consultants and procurement playbooks
- AI answers to "best IFM provider," "national HVAC for restaurants," etc.

If a channel is not on this list, justify it with a named buyer and a named
job before spending.
